# Research Scope

## Proposed Research Title

AI-Driven Emergency Medicine Availability Prediction and Smart Pharmacy Rebalancing Framework.

## Research Problem

During emergencies, users can find nearby pharmacies but often cannot know whether the required medicine will still be available by the time they arrive. Pharmacies also experience unexpected shortages because many inventory systems react after stock becomes low instead of predicting shortages in advance.

## Research Gap

Existing systems commonly address pharmacy search, inventory tracking, demand forecasting, or supply chain optimization separately. The proposed research focuses on combining predicted availability at arrival time, emergency-aware pharmacy ranking, and proactive inter-pharmacy stock rebalancing in one practical framework.

## Research Aim

To design and evaluate an AI-driven framework that predicts medicine availability at arrival time, ranks pharmacies by emergency suitability, and recommends stock redistribution to reduce predicted medicine shortages.

## Research Objectives

1. Develop a real-time pharmacy inventory and medicine search platform.
2. Build a machine learning model to predict medicine demand and stock-out risk.
3. Design an availability-on-arrival prediction model using inventory, demand, and travel-time features.
4. Develop an emergency rescue score to rank pharmacies based on reliability, urgency, and accessibility.
5. Build an optimization-based stock rebalancing module for nearby pharmacies.

## Research Questions

1. How accurately can medicine availability at user arrival time be predicted using inventory and demand patterns?
2. Does an availability-aware pharmacy ranking outperform nearest-pharmacy selection during emergency scenarios?
3. Which features most influence medicine stock-out risk and availability confidence?
4. Can optimization-based stock redistribution reduce predicted shortages across nearby pharmacies?

## Expected Contribution

The project contributes a practical emergency medicine access framework that integrates ML-based availability prediction, geospatial pharmacy ranking, and proactive stock rebalancing while maintaining medical safety boundaries.

## Applicability to Sri Lanka

The system can be adapted to Sri Lankan pharmacies by using locally relevant pharmacy locations, medicine names, stock update patterns, opening hours, and emergency demand assumptions. If real pharmacy inventory data is unavailable, realistic synthetic inventory data can be generated using sales velocity, seasonal demand, stock refill schedules, and pharmacy location density.
